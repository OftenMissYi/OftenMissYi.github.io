UAV Path Planning & Trajectory Control

From discrete 3D paths to smooth Minimum-Snap trajectories and closed-loop tracking in MATLAB.

Overview

Planning, Trajectory Generation & Control

A team course project at Chongqing University connecting geometric path planning with continuous-time UAV motion. Four stages cover path tracking, A*/Theta* planning, a dynamic height penalty and Minimum-Snap trajectory generation.

The work was implemented and evaluated in MATLAB simulation, using a provided PID controller to track the generated reference states.

I coordinated the overall team work and participated across all four tasks. My main technical focus was leading the implementation of the final Minimum-Snap trajectory generator and integrating its desired states with the provided tracking controller.

The implementation uses seventh-order polynomials and quadratic optimization to connect discrete waypoints with smooth trajectory segments. I also contributed to path-planning experiments, simulation evaluation and the consolidation of the final report.

Technical Approach

Four Stages of Development

01 · Reference Path Tracking

Allocate a 25-second trajectory duration in proportion to segment lengths, generate piecewise-linear position and velocity references, and derive yaw from the motion direction with protection near zero velocity.

02 · A* and Theta* Planning

Plan paths in a known 3D occupancy grid. A* uses six-neighbor connectivity; Theta* adds line-of-sight checks to connect nodes at arbitrary angles and reduce unnecessary grid-constrained turns.

03 · Dynamic Height Penalty

Add a penalty for deviation from a progress-dependent reference altitude. This shapes vertical motion while searching through the obstacle environment. The Theta* variant also penalizes large single-step vertical jumps.

04 · Minimum-Snap Trajectory Generation

Represent each segment with a seventh-order polynomial and minimize the integral of squared snap, the fourth derivative of position. Solve for coefficients with MATLAB's quadprog, subject to waypoint and inter-segment continuity constraints through jerk.

Optimization is performed during initialization. Subsequent time queries evaluate position, velocity and acceleration references for the controller, separating trajectory preparation from tracking.

Planning Results

A* / Theta* Comparison

In the report's complex-environment scenario, Theta* used fewer waypoints and a shorter path, at the cost of more planning time.

Integration

From Waypoints to Tracking References

The final stage connects the planner's waypoint output to the trajectory generator and the provided PID controller. Simulation plots document the resulting 3D motion and the desired versus actual position and velocity.

The repository includes MATLAB implementations for all four tasks, task presentations and the final report. The work demonstrates the planning-to-control pipeline in simulation; physical flight validation is outside this project.
